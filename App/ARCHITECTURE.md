## Milestone 2 architecture

### One engine, two stores

Every mutation is a `LedgerCommand` executed by the pure `LedgerEngine.perform(_:on:actor:now:makeID:)`. It
validates against a `LedgerSnapshot` and returns `[LedgerChange]` plus audit events, or throws without side
effects. `InMemoryLedgerStore` applies changes to a snapshot; `SwiftDataLedgerStore` applies the same changes
to records and saves once (all or nothing). This is how "both stores implement identical rules" (spec §14) is
guaranteed by construction rather than by duplicated code; `PersistenceParityTests` runs one script against
both stores and compares every entity.

Narrow repository facets sit on top: `GroupRepository`, `ExpenseRepository`, `SettlementRepository`,
`PaymentDestinationRepository`, `PaymentAttemptRepository`, `SampleDataManaging`. Feature views see only these;
SwiftData never reaches a view. The clock and ID source are injected so tests and UI tests are deterministic.

### Ledger model

| Concept | Representation | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Removed member | `Membership.removedAt` | Can't join new expenses or payments; history keeps them. Removal blocked while they have a balance or an open payment |
| Expense edit | New revision on the same record; previous `ExpenseRevision` stored in the `.expenseEdited` audit event | Only the current revision affects balances |
| Expense deletion | Tombstone (`deletedAt`, `deletedBy`) + `.expenseDeleted` event with the snapshot | `LedgerSnapshot.expenses` excludes tombstones, so every calculator ignores them automatically; undo = restore |
| Currency | Locked once a group has any expense, settlement, or payment attempt | — |
| `Settlement` | Unchanged meaning: a **confirmed** external payment. Added `provider`, `attemptID`, `confirmedAt`, `confirmedBy` | Created only by recipient confirmation (or direct/legacy recording) |
| `PaymentAttempt` | Transient lifecycle with full transition history (state, actor, time, note) | `PaymentAttemptMachine` enforces spec §13; repeats are idempotent no-ops |
| Audit log | Append-only `AuditEvent` with a monotonic sequence | Group, membership, expense, and payment events; rendered by `AuditEventPresenter` |

### Balances and the actionable plan

`SettlementPlanner.plan` returns both the **confirmed** nets (expenses − confirmed settlements) and the
**available** nets (confirmed, with `reportedSent`/`disputed` attempts reserved as if paid). Payments are
suggested from the available nets, so a reported payment is never suggested twice; rejection or cancellation
removes the reservation. Simplification: exact-match pairing, then greedy largest-debtor → largest-creditor;
ties break by membership order; at most n − 1 payments; property-tested to preserve every net.

Financially conflicting changes are refused (`pendingPaymentConflict`): an edit, deletion, restore, or new
expense that would change the balance of anyone with a reported or disputed payment in that group must wait
until the payment is confirmed, rejected, or cancelled. Reporting re-checks the obligation, so a stale draft
can't over-reserve.

### Payment handoffs (non-custodial)

- Destinations are validated per provider (`PaymentDestinationValidator`) and links must pass the HTTPS host
  allowlist (`ExternalURLPolicy`) — at entry, again in the engine, and again before opening.
- `HandoffPlanner` produces an honest plan per method: capability label (`Amount prepared` / `Opens provider` /
  `Copy and continue` / `Record manually`), action, copyable details, steps, warnings. Never "verified",
  "instant", or "secure" (tested).
- `PaymentHandoffService` is the only code that opens URLs or writes the pasteboard (local-only, expires after
  two minutes). Logs carry provider names and outcomes only.
- An attempt exists before anything leaves the app; opening another app records only `handoffOpened`; return
  prompts ask "Did you send …?" with three un-preselected choices. Unfinished handoffs persist and re-prompt on
  Home after relaunch.
- Identifiers are masked in general views and revealed only to the owner and to a payer with an active
  obligation. `DestinationValue` and `PaymentDestination` redact themselves in `description`/`debugDescription`.

| Provider | Handoff | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Cash | `MFMessageComposeViewController` to the recipient with a prepared message; amount copied; "Continue in Messages" | Apple provides no public API for Apple Cash amount, selection, sending, or status |
| PayPal | `https://paypal.me/<name>/<amount><CUR>` | PayPal.Me help documents amount and currency suffixes |
| Cash App | Opens the recipient-provided `https://cash.app/$…` link; otherwise copies $Cashtag, amount, memo | Spec §12: no constructed links |
| Zelle | Copy contact/amount/memo; validated QR link on `zelle.com`/`zellepay.com`; bank finder `https://www.zelle.com/get-started`; irreversibility warning | Zelle lives in bank apps; `zellepay.com` now redirects to `zelle.com` |
| Venmo | Guided copy of @username, amount, note | No current official P2P link documentation was found; `venmo.com/u/` appears only in unofficial sources |
| Cash / other | Instructions + report | — |

## Milestone 3 architecture — Receipt Intelligence and the Live Receipt Room

### One aggregate, one engine, one transaction

A receipt lives in a single `ReceiptRoom` aggregate from capture to finalization: the reviewed
`ReceiptDraft` is inside it, so a draft and an open room persist, reload, and migrate the same way.
Its `state` covers every lifecycle stage in the spec (`draft → ready → open ⇄ resolving → readyToFinalize →
finalized`, or `cancelled`). Every change is a `ReceiptRoomCommand` executed by `LedgerEngine` (as
`LedgerCommand.receipt`), so the in-memory and SwiftData stores share one implementation, exactly like
milestone 2. Roles, permissions, transitions, and revisions are enforced there:

| Rule | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stale commands refused | `expectedRevision` on every command → `staleRevision(current:)` | The device's own rapid taps are serialized (`ReceiptFlowModel.runInSequence`) and built against the latest room; editor sheets keep the revision their view was based on |
| Host-only | review, confirm, open, participants, charge strategies, dispute resolution, receipt revision, state moves, finalize, cancel, image removal | Identity switching sets only the *actor*; host rights never move |
| Participant | claim (Mine/Share), unclaim, custom split among existing claimants + self, dispute, mark reviewed | Only while `open`; in `resolving` only the host assigns |
| Finalization | `finalize` → same `addExpense` path as manual entry (validation, pending-payment conflicts) | Expense ID = room ID; a repeat is a no-op returning the same expense. Expense creation, room lock, snapshot, and audit events are one atomic save |
| Host edits after claims | `reviseReceipt` + `ReceiptRevisionImpact` | Only items whose amount changed or that were removed lose claims; previous items and claims go into history; affected people are flagged to review |

Audit: the room carries its own append-only history (`ReceiptRoomEvent`, with the actual simulated actor and
revision); the group log gains `receiptRoomOpened/Finalized/Cancelled`. The expense keeps the frozen
`ReceiptAllocationSnapshot` (receipt values, item and charge shares, participant totals, payments,
rounding, revision, time) via `LedgerSnapshot.room(forExpense:)`, so it stays explainable after edits.

### Recognition pipeline (on device, deterministic baseline)

`capture/import → normalize (ReceiptAssetStore + ImageIO) → recognize (VisionReceiptRecognizer,
RecognizeTextRequest .accurate) → structure (DeterministicReceiptStructurer) → validate (ReceiptValidator)
→ review (ReceiptReviewModel) → confirm (engine)`. Each stage has typed input/output; `ReceiptPipeline`
runs stages 3–5 `@concurrent` (off the main actor) with a cancellation check between stages and pages.
Vision types never leave the recognizer; the domain sees `RecognizedRegion` (text, normalized text,
confidence, top-left unit bounds, alternatives, reading order).

The structurer uses row geometry, decimal/currency patterns, quantity and unit-price patterns, summary
labels, date patterns, merchant placement, exclusion patterns (masked cards, auth codes, phones,
addresses, URLs, table/server text), and arithmetic consistency to rank competing totals. It never edits
a printed value: mismatches surface as the exact difference, and the person fixes a line or adds a
labelled adjustment. Every value is a `ReceiptField` (value, original proposal, attention, reason, source
regions, editor, time). Attention is shown as **Looks good / Check this / Needs resolution** with icon +
words.

The bundled sample receipt is recognized by `FixtureReceiptRecognizer` (prerecorded regions with one
deliberate misreading) so demos and UI tests are deterministic; camera and Photos pages always use Vision.
A Vision integration test runs real OCR on the same image with tolerant, semantic assertions.

### Allocation

`ReceiptAllocationEngine` divides every item and charge with `SplitCalculator.allocate` (integer minor
units, largest remainder, ties by room participant order). Negative lines (refunds, discounts) are divided
by magnitude and negated so rounding moves consistently. Charges: proportional to item subtotals
(default), equal (everyone or a chosen list), shares, exact (must add up), or payer. The result carries
per-person totals (items, tax, tip, fees, discounts, adjustments), rounding notes, and the remaining
amount; `ReceiptRoomReadiness` is the single readiness rule set used by the UI and by `finalize`
(unclaimed/partial/invalid items, unallocated charges, open disputes, receipt invalid, totals ≠ receipt,
group/payer/participants still active, no negative participant totals, payer-keeps-all confirmation).
Property tests prove conservation over random claims and strategies.

### Files and persistence

- Images are files in `Application Support/Receipts` (complete-unless-open protection, atomic writes):
  the untouched original, an oriented/rotated/cropped page variant (≤ 3200 px, used for OCR and display),
  and a 480 px thumbnail. SwiftData stores only `ReceiptAsset` references and metadata (inside the room payload).
- `ReceiptRoomRecord(id, groupID, state, expenseID, updatedAt, payload)` — JSON payload like milestone 2's records.
- **Schema versioning:** `SquareUpSchemaV2` (the milestone 2 models as shipped) → `SquareUpSchemaV3` (+ `ReceiptRoomRecord`)
  through `SquareUpMigrationPlan` (lightweight, additive). `ReceiptPersistenceTests.migrationFromMilestone2`
  builds a store with exactly the V2 schema, then opens it through the plan and verifies every entity.
- Orphan cleanup at launch removes only files matching the store's own naming pattern that no room
  references and that are older than 10 minutes. Discarding a draft deletes its files; Reset Sample Data
  deletes receipt files only.
- **Retention:** finalized receipt images stay with the expense, including after the expense is deleted
  (deletion is a restorable tombstone). The host can remove the images explicitly ("Remove receipt
  images"); the allocation snapshot and history remain, and the viewer shows "Images removed".

### Interface

Full-screen flow (`ReceiptFlowView`) whose step is derived from the persisted room state, so a relaunch
resumes at the right screen; drafts and open rooms are listed on Home, in the group, and in the source
chooser. Screens: source chooser (Scan with Camera / Choose from Photos / sample, on-device privacy note),
VisionKit document camera (edge detection, perspective correction, retake, multi-page), PhotosPicker (no
library permission), named processing stages with cancel, review (zoomable page with the selected value's
evidence highlighted, live reconciliation, every field editable, merge/split/move/delete lines, page
rotate/crop/replace/reorder/remove), reconciliation detail, room setup, the room (identity switcher,
running totals, filters All/Mine/Unclaimed/Shared/Disputed, Mine/Share/Undo/Custom split/Dispute), shared-
item editor (equal/shares/exact with live "adds up" and rounding disclosure), charge allocation editor,
dispute editor and host resolution, final review (per-person breakdown, rounding, adjustments, proof
statement), success (mesh + `settle` haptic, only after finalization), and the itemized section on expense
detail (receipt viewer, item and charge shares, totals, proof, room history). Haptics: selection on claims,
warning on refused actions or a new mismatch, `reconcile` when the room becomes fully assigned, `settle`
on finalization. VoiceOver announces each claim's effect on the acting person's total and every change in
reconciliation; every gesture (zoom, crop, reorder) has a button or adjustable alternative.

### App Clip, SharePlay, Live Activities (designed, not shipped)

- **App Clip:** invocation `https://<domain>/r/<room-id>` → join screen with a lightweight identity (display name,
  no account) → the same `ReceiptRoomView` driven by a remote `ReceiptRoomRepository`. The room UI already
  takes its data from a repository and commands, so it is App-Clip-compatible; it is *not* built because
  a local-only clip would misrepresent invitations (§3.5). Temporary participants would be stored as
  room-scoped identities that expire with the room.
- **SharePlay:** presence/coordination only, layered on the authoritative server room (§3.6).
- **Live Activity:** would show only "room open / N items left / action needed / ready to finalize"; never item
  names or amounts (§3.7). Deferred.
- **Foundation Models:** `ReceiptStructuring` is the provider boundary; a model-backed structurer can be
  added, availability-checked, with the pipeline falling back to the deterministic structurer on any
  error. Not shipped in M3 (optional; see deviations).

## Milestone 4 architecture — shared Live Receipt Rooms

### The server is authoritative; the room screens are unchanged

`HW/server` (Flask on App Engine, Firestore in Datastore mode, private Cloud Storage; see `server/README.md` and
`server/API.md`) owns shared rooms. It holds a Python port of the receipt engine (allocation, readiness, commands),
and **parity fixtures** (`HW/Shared/parity-fixtures`) prove the two engines agree. They run in
`server/tests/test_parity.py` and `SquareUpTests/Milestone4/ParityFixtureTests.swift`.

The full app shows a shared room with the **same screens** as a local one. `RemoteRoomLedger` conforms to
`LedgerStore`, so `ReceiptFlowModel` and every room view work unchanged. The container swaps in an
`AppEnvironment.shared(session)` once a room is published or joined:

- `snapshot()` builds a *workspace* from the server snapshot (`RemoteRoomMapper.workspace`). The workspace holds
  the room, its participants as members, and a group made of exactly those participants, which is the same
  context the server uses for readiness.
- `perform(.receipt(command))` translates the command (`RemoteRoomMapper.command`) and sends it with a command
  ID, an idempotency key, and the revision it was based on. It returns only when the server accepts or refuses it.
- **Optimistic previews.** These are applied on top of the server snapshot by the same Swift engine and marked
  pending (`pendingItemIDs` → "Sending…" on the row, "Sending N changes…" in the status bar). Every preview is
  removed when the server answers, so nothing looks confirmed early (§7.4).
- **Serial commands.** Commands run one at a time, and a person's own quick taps are *rebased* past revisions
  that only this device produced, so they never conflict with each other.
- **Conflicts (§7.2).** The server refuses a stale revision atomically and returns the current snapshot. The
  preview is dropped, the latest room is shown, and `SharedRoomStatusBar` explains it with "Try again", which
  resends the same intent on the new revision with a new key.
- **Offline.** Unknown outcomes (offline, timeout, 5xx, 429) are retried twice with the *same* envelope. If
  they still fail, the change is kept as *unsent* with "Send again" (same idempotency key, so it is never
  applied twice), and the room shows "Offline · showing <time>". Changes are not queued across relaunches.
  A change is either confirmed or visibly unsent.
- **Polling (§7.3).** Polling runs only while the room is on screen and the scene is active, refreshes
  immediately on return to the foreground, never overlaps a command, backs off 3 s → 30 s after failures,
  doesn't redraw when nothing changed, and stops at finalized or cancelled rooms or when access ends.

### Identity (§8)

- **Full app: Firebase Authentication** (decided with the course owner). Hosting creates an anonymous Firebase
  account, which is a stable, server-verified user ID with no sign-up. Profile → Shared rooms can upgrade it in
  place to email and password (same UID), which allows account recovery on another iPhone. The server verifies
  ID tokens (`google-auth`) and never trusts client roles. Firebase is reached through its REST API (no SDK;
  see `DEPENDENCIES.md`). The refresh token is stored in the Keychain.
- **App Clip guests:** a 256-bit, room-scoped guest credential returned once by `join`. It is stored hashed on the
  server, with a 72-hour sliding expiry, and is revoked when the host removes the guest or the guest links an
  account (`link-guest`). A display name is never identity.
- **Development:** `Dev <install id>` against a local server started with `SQUAREUP_ALLOW_DEV_AUTH=1`. This
  works in DEBUG builds only, and the server refuses it in production and on App Engine.
- Configuration comes from `Config/Remote.xcconfig`, which is committed with the production **client**
  configuration — the API host and Firebase's public client identifiers — so a fresh clone builds and runs the
  full app (sign-in, Demo Mode, shared groups, live rooms, AI receipt reading) with no setup. These values ship
  inside every copy of the app and grant nothing by themselves; every privileged secret stays on the server
  (the Anthropic key in Secret Manager, deployment settings in the uncommitted `server/deploy.env`), and the
  server decides access. An optional, gitignored `Remote.local.xcconfig` overrides any value (a local server,
  another Firebase project). UI tests and unit tests run with sharing off (`-SUUITesting`). A launch that
  somehow has no server configuration logs a fault rather than passing silently as an offline build.

### Group ledgers stay on each device

Only the room travels. Seats come from the host's group (their `MemberID`s are the participant IDs), and
invitees can claim a seat or join as a new guest. When the shared room is finalized, the host's device records
the expense once through `ReceiptRoomCommand.adoptShared`:

1. The host first matches each guest to a group member, or adds them to the group (`GuestMatchingSheet`).
   Finalizing is blocked until every guest who owes something is matched.
2. Guest IDs are remapped.
3. The local room is finalized by the existing engine path.
4. The command **refuses if the app's totals differ from the server's finalization**
   (`ReceiptRoomError.sharedTotalsDiffer`).

The command is idempotent (`ExpenseID(room.id)`), so polls and relaunches never create a second expense.
Participants see a read-only "Final split" with their share.

### Invitations and links

The host's `InviteSheet` shows a QR code (Core Image) and a share link, `https://<server>/r/<token>`. The token
is 256 random bits, returned once, and kept in the host's Keychain so the sheet can show it again. The sheet
can also make a new link (revoking the old one) or turn links off.

The full app opens invitations from:

- universal links (once Associated Domains exist),
- `squp://join/<token>` (the `/r/` page offers this link),
- a pasted link (`JoinWithLinkSheet`).

`JoinRoomView` previews the room (host, merchant, date, open seats only) before joining.
`SharedRoomDirectory` (a small JSON file with no secrets) lists rooms this device hosts or joined.

## Milestone 5 architecture — hybrid AI receipt intelligence

Contract: `HW/milestone 5.md`. The App Clip stays paused; nothing here touches it.

### Pipeline

```text
capture/import → normalize (unchanged) → Vision OCR (always, on device)
  → Understanding items:
      .cloud route  → CloudReceiptStructurer → POST /v1/receipt-intelligence/parse
                      → server: strip metadata, alias regions, Claude (structured outputs), strict schema,
                        deterministic validation → ReceiptIntelligenceMapper (re-validates on device)
      any failure / .onDevice route → DeterministicReceiptStructurer (milestone 3, unchanged)
  → Checking totals → review (ReceiptValidator, unchanged rules + two new blocking rules) → confirm
```

- **Provider boundary.** `ReceiptStructuring` from milestone 3 stays; the pipeline takes a
  `ReceiptStructuringRoute` (`.cloud(CloudReceiptStructurer)` or `.onDevice(reason)`). The cloud
  structurer throws a `ReceiptIntelligenceFallback` (12 classes) for every failure; the pipeline then
  runs the deterministic structurer and records the class in `RecognitionInfo.fallback`. Success
  records `RecognitionInfo.cloud` (schema version, cached, duration, overall confidence) and
  provider `.cloudModel`. Both fields are optional, so earlier drafts decode unchanged.
- **Routing** (`ReceiptFlowModel.structuringRoute`): no server in the build → `.onDevice(nil)`
  (exactly milestone 3); `onDeviceOnly` → `.onDevice(.onDeviceOnly)`; undecided → the disclosure
  sheet first, and dismissing it gives `.onDevice(.notConsented)`; automatic but known offline →
  `.onDevice(.offline)` without a network attempt; otherwise `.cloud`.
- **Transport.** `ReceiptIntelligenceAPI` uses the existing `AccountSession` credential (Authorization
  header only) on its own ephemeral `URLSession` (65 s request / 70 s resource; the room session's
  30 s would cut AI processing off). The structurer adds a 60 s deadline. A `409 ai_in_progress` is
  waited for (up to three times), never re-sent as a new charge.
- **Processing image.** `ReceiptImageProcessing.processingJPEG` re-encodes the oriented, cropped page
  from a bare `CGImage` (no EXIF/GPS/TIFF), with long edge ≤ 2576 px and ⌈w/28⌉·⌈h/28⌉ ≤ 4784
  (Claude's documented high-resolution limits), quality 0.82, ≤ 6 MB. The server strips APPn/COM
  segments again.
- **Mapping and validation.** `ReceiptIntelligenceMapper` accepts only schema version 1, ISO-shaped
  currency codes, bounded integer minor units, known attention states and charge kinds, and region IDs
  the app actually sent (others are dropped). New `AttentionReason`s: `derived`, `evidenceMismatch`
  (Claude read a value Vision didn't see at the cited place, e.g. the sample's $8.50 vs OCR's $6.50),
  `noEvidence`. `ReceiptValidator` gains blocking `itemNeedsResolution` / `chargeNeedsResolution`
  (an amount that couldn't be read: `needsResolution` + `notFound`) and `totalNeedsConfirmation` (a total
  worked out rather than printed: `needsResolution` + `derived`), cleared by editing or confirming the value.
  Both are keyed to reasons only AI processing produces, so receipts built from server data (shared rooms,
  parity fixtures) are unaffected — the parity suite caught an earlier, broader version of this rule. Claude's original values stay in
  `ReceiptField.proposed` / `ItemProposal` (on-device audit) and are never logged.
- **Consent.** `ReceiptAISettings` (`UserDefaults`, versioned "automatic.v1" / "onDeviceOnly"),
  `ReceiptAIConsentSheet` (two equal choices), Profile → Receipts. `AppEnvironment.setReceiptAIMode`
  deletes the account's server cache when consent is withdrawn. Tests, previews, and UI tests keep
  the choice in memory.
- **Experience.** Stages: Preparing receipt, Reading text, **Understanding items**, Checking totals.
  The processing copy says AI is used only when it is. Review shows "Items identified with AI" or the
  calm fallback line "AI processing wasn't available. We prepared this receipt on your device."
  VoiceOver hears every stage change and the result with the number of problems
  (`ReceiptFlowModel.announcements` → `AccessibilityNotification.Announcement`). Processing holds a
  background-task assertion; if time runs out it is cancelled and the saved draft remains.

### Server

`squareup/intelligence/` (settings + `Secret`, schema, prompt, images, validation, provider,
anthropic_provider, metrics) and `services/intelligence.py`; see `server/README.md` and `server/API.md`.
The provider is chosen once at startup (`select_provider`). It is off by default. In production,
Claude's key is read once from Google Secret Manager (`SQUAREUP_ANTHROPIC_SECRET`, a resource name),
CRC32C-verified, shape-checked, and kept only in a `Secret`. Any failure stops the app from starting,
logging only a reason code. Locally, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is still accepted. Production also refuses
the recorded provider.

Paid parsing requires a Firebase account whose token says `email_verified: true` (`403
email_not_verified` otherwise). The daily limits are configurable and start conservative: 10 provider
calls per verified account and 50 across the project. Cache hits don't count.

### Evaluation

`HW/Shared/receipt-eval`: 20 fictional receipts rendered to JPEGs with real Vision OCR evidence;
`ReceiptEvaluationTests` scores deterministic, Claude, and hybrid strategies; the opt-in
`server/scripts/run_receipt_eval.py` collects live Claude results.

## P2P scope and legacy compatibility (October 2026)

`LegacyRecords` (`Services/Persistence/LegacyRecords.swift`) holds the rules, applied every time

- **Legacy organization groups are excluded**, together with their expenses, settlements, payment attempts,

- **Any expense carrying legacy organization funding is excluded** wherever it is found, for the same reason.
- **Nothing a person created is deleted.** Legacy records stay on disk, untouched and inactive.
- **Only the retired sample is deleted.** `removeRetiredSampleData()` runs at launch after seeding. It deletes

  methods stay because they also belong to personal groups.
- A store already seeded by an earlier build keeps its personal sample groups. It gains the new sample group
  only through Profile → Reset sample data, which seeds personal groups only.

The in-memory store and every calculator work only on domain types, so there is no compatibility code
outside persistence. None of this has been exercised against a real earlier-build store yet. That belongs to
the separate testing and validation task.

## Milestone 7 architecture — real multi-user P2P groups

Contract: `HW/milestone 7.md`. Still P2P only; no organization features. The App Clip, Associated Domains, and
AASA stay untouched.

### Where each group lives

There are now two kinds of group, chosen in Create group (**On this iPhone** / **Shared**):

- **Local groups** work exactly as before through `SwiftDataLedgerStore`.
- **Shared groups** live on the server. Each one is a `SharedGroupLedger`: a `LedgerStore` for one group, so
  every existing screen (group detail, compose, settle-up, paying, payment attempt, activity, receipts) runs
  on it unchanged. `AppEnvironment.environment(for:)` routes a screen to the right store by group ID. Home and
  Activity merge local and shared summaries (`HomeSummary`, `ActivityView`).

There is still one ledger engine. `SharedGroupLedger` builds a per-group workspace
(`SharedGroupMapper.workspace`) from the server's snapshot, with this account's seat as `currentUserID`, and
runs `LedgerEngine` on it to validate commands and preview their effect. The server runs a Python port of the
same rules (`server/squareup/ledger/`) and is authoritative. The ledger parity fixtures
(`Shared/parity-fixtures/ledger`, 3 scenarios: multi-payer and split methods, delete/restore/edit
revisions, payment reporting/dispute/confirm) prove that both sides reach the same outcome and the same
balances after every step.

### Commands, revisions, and sync

- Every change is a `GroupCommandEnvelope` (command ID, idempotency key, expected revision, known cursor).
  Owner and membership commands are **revision-strict**: a stale revision is refused and the person reviews
  the current state. Expense commands are **rebased**: the server revalidates them against the current
  state. Editing an expense someone else changed in the meantime is refused (`expense_conflict`).
- **Optimistic previews only for expense commands** (add, edit, delete, restore). Payment confirmation,
  rejection, dispute, membership, ownership, archive, leave, and receipt finalize/adopt are **live-only**:
  never queued, and shown only after the server accepts them. A reported payment shows "Waiting for …" only
  after the server stored it.
- An expense change made offline is queued in the account's cache with the **same idempotency key**, so
  resending can apply it at most once. Changes the server refuses after reconnecting stay in the group's status bar with the
  reason until the person discards them.
- Sync reads `GET /changes?after=<cursor>` (gap detection; a gap or `cursorExpired` falls back to a full
  snapshot). Polling is quick while a group screen or a payment involving this person is open, slower
  otherwise, with backoff. An app-wide loop refreshes the group list and payments awaiting confirmation for
  Home. There are no push notifications in this milestone.
- After applying server state, the client recomputes balances and compares them with the server's
  (`SharedGroupMapper.balancesAgree`). A mismatch keeps the last good version and says "Couldn't read the latest changes"
  instead of guessing.
- Equal splits use exact `Int128` distribution (`SplitCalculator`), and the original split input (weights or
  percentages) travels with the expense (`Expense.splitInput`, transient), so the server recomputes the same
  allocation instead of trusting it.

### Accounts and identity

`AccountSession` adds email/password recovery to the anonymous Firebase account (same user ID, so hosted
receipt rooms stay theirs), sign-in on another iPhone, password reset (answered the same whether or not the
address exists), and sign-out. Shared groups require a recoverable account; the server refuses anonymous
tokens with `account_required`. Profile → Account shows the state, the name people see, and what
signing out removes. `ProfileDestinationsLedger` keeps payment destinations on the account.

`SharedGroupCache` keeps the last confirmed snapshot of each group, the unsent queue, and local receipt
drafts **per account** (directory named by a hash of the account ID, complete-unless-open protection,
excluded from backup). Signing out deletes that account's directory. Nothing in the cache is authoritative.

### Invitations

The owner's `GroupInviteSheet` shows a code `XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX` (100 bits), a QR code, Copy, and Share.
The shared link is `https://<server>/g/<code>`: an HTTPS page that shows the code and an "Open Square Up"
button (`squp://join-group/<code>`). It is **not** a universal link, and the app doesn't claim it is.
`JoinGroupView` takes a pasted code or link and shows a safe preview (group name, inviter, member count,
open seats) before joining. An invitation can be tied to a seat (a member the owner added before inviting);
accepting binds that seat to the account once.

### Publishing a local group

`PublishGroupSheet` reviews a local group (`SharedGroupsService.review`) and blocks publication on anything
that can't be represented safely. Blockers: payments in progress, unfinished receipts, expenses that don't
validate, currency mismatches, duplicate IDs. The sheet also lists what will be shared. The request uses a
stable publication ID kept in the account cache, so a retry after a lost response returns the same group
instead of creating a second one. Legacy organization records are never in the snapshot (`LegacyRecords`),
so they can't be uploaded. Payment drafts, receipt images, and payment methods stay on the iPhone. Once the
server confirms, the local group is set aside by marking `GroupRecord.kind = "publishedShared"`. That needs
no schema change. Nothing is deleted, and the store excludes the group from local reads.

### Payments

The payment flow is the milestone 2 flow on the shared ledger. Destination details come from
`GET /members/<id>/destinations`, which the server answers only while the caller owes that member.
Opening a provider app proves nothing. `reportedSent` is the payer's statement and reserves the amount.
**Only the recipient's confirmation on their own account creates the settlement**, exactly once. Home's
"Confirm received" for a shared group goes to that group's ledger with the recipient's seat as the actor.

## Sign-in, email verification, and first run (October 2026)

A build with Firebase configured (production) now opens through an account flow instead of sample
data. Builds without Firebase (local demos, a development server, previews, the historical UI tests)
skip it and open as before.

### One state machine

`AuthCoordinator` (`Features/Auth`) owns a single `AuthPhase`, and `AuthGate` shows exactly one screen
for it. `RootView` exists only in `.ready`, so the app can't flash before the session is resolved.

| Phase | Screen | Leaves when |
|---|---|---|
| `restoring` | Launch (brand + "Checking your account…", a slow-network note after 8 s) | the stored session is checked |
| `signedOut` | Welcome, with Sign in / Create account / Reset password pushed (`AuthRoute`) | an account signs in or is created |
| `verifyingEmail` | Check your email | Firebase reports `emailVerified` |
| `needsProfile` | What should we call you? | the server profile is saved |
| `onboarding(.paymentMethods)` | How friends pay you (optional) | Continue / Set up later |
| `onboarding(.finished)` | You're all set | Start using Square Up |
| `ready` | the app | Sign out, or the session ends |
| `unavailable` | Can't reach Square Up (Retry / Sign out) | retry succeeds |

**Launch.** No stored account means Welcome, with no network request. A stored email account
refreshes its token and reads the account (`accounts:lookup`). The outcomes:

- **Refresh token refused** (any 4xx from `securetoken`): the session ends. The account is signed
  out, and sign-in opens prefilled with the email and an explanation.
- **Offline or service error:** the app doesn't sign anyone out. It continues from what the iPhone
  remembers:
  - an unverified account goes to verification (with an "offline" note);
  - a verified account with a saved profile opens the app with its saved data;
  - otherwise the flow shows `unavailable`.

**Requests.** They run one at a time (`isWorking`), so a double tap can't create two accounts.
Results that arrive after the person moved on (for example, a verification check after "Use a
different email") are ignored. Token refreshes are shared: concurrent requests wait on one refresh
(`AccountSession.validIDToken`).

### Firebase (REST for email; the SDK only for phone verification — see "Phone sign-in")

`AccountSession` gained these calls:

- `sendEmailVerification` (`accounts:sendOobCode`, `VERIFY_EMAIL`);
- `lookup` (`accounts:lookup`, the only trusted source for `emailVerified`);
- `restore`;
- `setDisplayName` (`accounts:update`, so profile setup can prefill the name after a relaunch);
- `passwordPolicy` (`v2/passwordPolicy`).

It no longer creates anonymous accounts on demand. Without an account, requests fail with
`signedOut`. An anonymous account left by an earlier build is upgraded in place when its owner creates
an account (`accounts:update`, same user ID), so receipt rooms it hosts stay theirs.

Alongside the refresh token, the Keychain record keeps the last confirmed `emailVerified`, the
creation date, and whether a profile exists. All three are optional, so older records still decode.
Logs carry only Firebase's error code.

**Verification policy** (`VerificationPolicy`):

- Every account must have a verified identity before the app opens: an address Firebase reports as
  verified, or a phone number Firebase verified with a code ("Phone sign-in"). There is
  no exception by account age (the earlier date cutoff for the original test accounts was removed
  when those accounts were deleted, October 2026).
- A Keychain record with no recorded verification state isn't trusted: offline, it waits at "Check
  your email" and is checked again once online.
- The server enforces the same rule on every account, shared-group, receipt-room, and
  receipt-intelligence route (see `server/API.md`, Authentication).

The verification link opens Firebase's hosted page. The app re-checks when it returns to the
foreground (at most every 5 s) and when the person taps "I've verified my email". It never polls in
the background. Resends wait out a 60 s cooldown that survives a relaunch, and Firebase's own rate
limit also starts one. "Open Mail" uses Apple Mail's `message://` scheme and falls back to written
instructions if it can't open.

**Passwords.** Square Up asks for at least 8 characters (Firebase's built-in floor is 6). If the
project enforces a Firebase password policy, its rules are added and shown as a checklist with
symbols and words. Password reset uses Firebase's emailed link and hosted page. The confirmation reads
the same whether or not the address has an account, and wrong-email and wrong-password failures share
one message.

### Profile and the local ledger

The name step saves the server profile (`PUT /v1/account`), then `LocalIdentityPreparing` makes the
person the current user of this iPhone's ledger:

- An empty ledger gets one member with the profile's member ID.
- A ledger that already has a current user keeps it and its groups.

A real account therefore starts with an empty, personalized Home. Builds that require an account never
seed sample data, and they hide "Explore sample groups", "Reset sample data", and the prototype
identity switcher, because each of those would replace the person's own ledger.

### Invitations through sign-in

`squp://join-group/…`, `/g/…`, `squp://join/…`, and `/r/…` links go to `AuthCoordinator.receive`.
Before the app is ready, the invitation waits in the Keychain (`PendingInvitationStore`, so it
survives a relaunch) and every auth screen says it's waiting. When the phase becomes `ready`, it is
handed to the shared-group or receipt-room join sheet exactly once.

### Signing out and ended sessions

Profile → Account shows the email (Verified / Not verified), the name, Change name, and Sign out. The
sign-out confirmation names any shared-group changes that haven't been sent yet (they're kept and
sent after signing in again). Signing out:

- closes the account's local space (sync, polling, and room sessions stop);
- clears the Keychain credentials and the pending invitation;
- returns to Welcome.

The account's local data, including its shared-group cache and queued changes, stays in its own
namespace (see "Multiple accounts on one iPhone"). If the server or Firebase refuses the session
while the app is open,
`RootView` hands it back to the coordinator, which returns to sign-in with an explanation.

### Firebase Console (owner)

Nothing new is required for the flow to work: Email/Password sign-in is already enabled, and Firebase's
default verification and reset email templates are used. Recommended, optional:

- **Templates:** set the sender name and template text in Authentication → Templates.
- **Anonymous provider:** leave it enabled so anonymous accounts from earlier builds keep refreshing
  until they're upgraded.
- **Email enumeration protection:** keep it on. Sign-in already reads the same for an unknown
  address and a wrong password.
- **Password policy:** set one in Authentication → Settings → Password policy if desired. The app
  reads it, and never asks for less than 8 characters.

Opening the verification link directly in Square Up would need a custom action URL plus Associated
Domains (paused). It is documented, not faked.

### Testing without Firebase

`DemoAuthBackend` (DEBUG only, `-SUAuthDemo <scenario>`) stands in for Firebase and the account
endpoint in UI tests. The scenarios are `fresh`, `unverified`, `noProfile`, `signedIn`,
`sessionEnded`, and `offline`. Unit tests use `FakeAuthBackend`, and REST tests use a scripted
transport. No automated test creates a Firebase user or sends email. In UI-test mode only, the
sign-up password fields use `.oneTimeCode` instead of `.newPassword`: with automated typing, the
simulator's Strong Password AutoFill takes over the first of two password fields.

## Multiple accounts on one iPhone (October 2026 hardening)

Every signed-in Firebase account has its own local namespace, so several people can sign in on one
iPhone without seeing each other's data.

### Account scopes

- **Namespace.** `AccountNamespace` is a one-way hash of the immutable Firebase UID
  (`SHA-256("squareup.account.v1:" + uid)`, first 32 hex characters). It is never the email address.
  Directories, preference suites, and Keychain services use it; logs show only `account-<8 chars>`.
- **Scope.** `AccountScope` holds everything personal for one account:
  - its SwiftData ledger (`Accounts/<ns>/Ledger.store`), with local groups, expenses, settlements,
    activity, receipt drafts and rooms, the person's identity, and payment methods;
  - receipt images (`Accounts/<ns>/Receipts/`) and the receipt-room directory
    (`Accounts/<ns>/SharedRooms.json`);
  - room invitation secrets (Keychain service `…credentials.<ns>`);
  - the AI-consent choice (defaults suite `…account.<ns>`);
  - its own `SharingService` and `SharedGroupsService`.

  Shared-group caches, cursors, and queued changes were already stored per account
  (`SharedGroups/<hash>/`). Device-level preferences (haptics, on-device AI) stay device-wide.
- **Session.** `AppSession` owns at most one open scope. Builds that require an account start with
  none: the signed-out screens run on an environment holding no personal data. A scope opens only
  after Firebase confirms the UID (`LiveAuthBackend`).
  - Opening another account first closes the current one: sync, polling, and room sessions stop,
    and the scope (with its SwiftData container) is released.
  - The app's screens exist only while a scope is open, and are keyed by `AppSession.generation`.
  - The router resets, and App Intents read the open scope through the session (nothing while
    signed out).
- **Signing out** closes the scope and forgets the Firebase credentials. It **keeps** the account's
  data, including shared-group changes still waiting to be sent, in its namespace. Signing back in
  restores it; nobody else is offered it.
- **Pending invitations** stay device-wide in the Keychain and contain only the code or token. They
  open in whichever account finishes signing in.

### Data from earlier builds (legacy claim)

Earlier builds kept one unscoped copy (`SquareUp.store`, `Receipts/`, `SharedRooms.json`).
`LegacyDataRegistry` records, durably and once, who owns it (`Accounts/legacy-claim.json`, hashes
only):

- At the first launch of this build, if the earlier store holds groups or expenses, the account
  remembered in this iPhone's Keychain becomes the candidate. That includes an anonymous account,
  which keeps its UID when it upgrades.
- When the candidate signs in and its own space is empty, the claim is written atomically, then the
  earlier files are opened as that account's ledger. The files are never moved, copied, or deleted.
  A failed write leaves the data unclaimed (the account opens its own empty space), and the next
  sign-in tries again.
- Any other account, or every account when no candidate is known, gets a choice. It shows counts
  only: "Use with this account", "It's not mine" (remembered per account), or "Decide later"
  (Profile → Earlier data on this iPhone).
  - An account that already has its own groups can't absorb the earlier data, because that would
    hide its own.
  - Once claimed, the data is never offered to anyone else.
- New accounts start empty.

### Verification is required for everyone

There's no in-app verification path any more: an unverified account, with or without a profile,
stops at "Check your email" (check, Open Mail, resend with a cooldown, use a different email,
delete an incomplete sign-up, sign out). A verified address triggers a forced ID-token refresh, so
`email_verified` reaches the server at once. Profile still shows the account's real state.

### Deleted accounts

When Firebase reports the stored account gone (`USER_NOT_FOUND`, a refused refresh token), the
launch flow ends the session: credentials are cleared, the account's scope is closed (its data
unloaded from memory), and the app returns to sign-in. A network failure never clears anything.
The account's files stay in its own UID namespace on the device, unreadable by any other account;
deleting the app removes them.

### Unverified sign-ups

"Use a different email" uses Firebase's verify-before-update (`VERIFY_AND_CHANGE_EMAIL`). The new
address gets a link, and the account switches to it, already verified, only when the link is opened.
Nothing is deleted, and a failure leaves the account unchanged.

"Delete this sign-up and start over" deletes only the current account (`accounts:delete`) after
confirmation. It refuses when the account is verified or already has a server profile. A
`CREDENTIAL_TOO_OLD_LOGIN_AGAIN` answer asks the person to sign in again, and an already-deleted
account counts as deleted, so a retry after a lost response is safe.

## Multi-person shared groups (October 2026)

This extends the milestone 7 shared group; it doesn't run a second group system. The same
aggregate, commands, events, invitations, and `SharedGroupLedger` sync carry everything below.

### The server's rules

- **Membership.** A group starts with its creator alone and holds up to 25 active members
  (`MAX_ACTIVE_MEMBERS`, separate from the 50-seat ceiling that still bounds legacy places).
  There is one owner, one currency, and one active membership per account per group. Joining
  uses the account's profile and immutable UID and never touches earlier expenses.
- **Leaving.** Leave, remove, and archive need a zero net and no payment in progress. Transfer is
  owner-only and recorded. Former members keep their attribution and get `not_a_member`.
- **Expenses.** The owner may delete or restore someone else's expense; the event is marked
  `byOwner`. Only the creator edits.
- **Verification.** Every account and group route except the status read needs
  `email_verified: true`.

### Invitations

"Invite people" makes a new single-use invitation each time; "Make another invitation" makes the
next. The server returns the code once and stores its digest only. The owner's iPhone keeps the
link in the Keychain (`SharedGroupsService+Invitations`, keyed by account UID and invitation ID), so
an unused invitation's QR and code can be shown again. A link is forgotten when the invitation is
seen accepted, expired, or revoked, and no other account on the iPhone can read it.

The owner's list comes from `GET …/invitations` with a server-computed `status`.
`GroupInvitationDTO.state(at:)` derives the same state for older servers, and treats an unused
invitation whose time ran out as expired. Seat invitations from groups published before this keep
working; the People screen lists those places separately as "Places waiting for someone".

### Screens

- **People** (`SharedGroupMembersSection`, derived by `GroupPeople`) shows these sections:
  - the owner, then everyone else with how they stand ("Owes $30.00", "Is owed …", "Settled up",
    "· payment in progress");
  - waiting places;
  - invitations waiting to be accepted, and accepted, expired, or turned-off ones, kept apart;
  - past members.

  The owner's options per person are Make owner and Remove; other members get Leave. The owner is
  told to hand over ownership before leaving. Each action is confirmed and waits for the server's
  answer.
- **Invitation** (`GroupInviteSheet`) has two modes. It either makes a new invitation (QR, code,
  copy, share, make another), or shows an existing one: its state, when it was made, when it
  expires, who accepted it, and Turn off.
- **Creating a shared group** shows only you ("Just you for now"). Nobody is added by name, and the
  editor of an existing shared group doesn't add people either.
- **Join preview** adds the owner's name and the invitation's expiry.
- **Expense detail** applies `ExpensePermissions`. In a shared group, only the creator sees Edit.
  The creator or the owner sees Delete and Restore, and the owner's confirmation says they're
  removing someone else's expense.

### Settling up

The existing planner suggests the payments, and each payer reports theirs; Square Up never says
it moved money. Only the recipient confirms, and an unrelated member is refused. A payment can be
for part of what's owed (up to the planned obligation); the rest stays suggested.

## Receipt split (October 2026)

After review, a receipt goes one of two ways (`ChoosePathView`); the review no longer asks for a
group or payer. Neither path adds a second room, invitation, settlement, or allocation system: both
are the receipt room's engine with different reach.

### Add to a group

`AddToGroupView` picks the group, the people who were there, and who paid.

- **A shared group**: `SharedGroupLedger.postReceipt` sends a `postReceipt` group command. The
  receipt becomes a pending allocation (`GroupReceiptDTO`) that travels with the group's snapshot and
  change pages, so every device sees it through normal sync. `GroupReceiptsSection` on the group
  screen and the Home banner ("Receipt needs your review") are the attention states.
- **Opening one**: `receiptSession` wraps it in a `RemoteRoomLedger` whose channel is a
  `GroupReceiptChannel`. Room commands become `receiptCommand`, finalize becomes `finalizeReceipt`,
  and cancel becomes `cancelReceipt`, all group commands with the receipt's own `expectedRevision`.
  The room screens are unchanged; `receipt_conflict` is read as `revision_conflict`.
- **Finalizing** records exactly one itemized expense, with the receipt's ID. Until then the
  receipt is never part of balances or the plan. Nobody gets a live link, QR, or code, and the photo
  stays on the host's phone.
- **A group on this iPhone** opens the on-device room as before ("Open room on this iPhone").

### Split with people now

`SplitNowView` publishes a temporary room (`SharingService.startTemporaryRoom`, `kind: "temporary"`).

- The scanner is the payer. They choose which of their payment methods to show and whether to share
  the photo, which is off by default and then uploaded privately.
- `InviteSheet` shows the QR code, link, and short code, more invitations, and turning them off.
  Saved invitations are kept per room in the Keychain.
- Guests join from Home → Join a receipt (`JoinWithLinkSheet`: link or code, parsed by
  `InvitationInput` in `SquareUpShared`) or the link's `squp://join/…` button.
- After finalization `TemporaryRoomSettleView` replaces the success screen: payments for the payer
  to confirm or reject, and what you owe, the payer's methods, and "I paid" for everyone else. It
  ends "All square", closed, or expired, and stays readable until retention ends.

### Shared rules

- **Units.** "2 of 3 beers": `ClaimMode.units` and `ItemSplit.units`, with what's left unclaimed
  (`UnitsClaimSheet`, `SharedItemEditor`'s "By quantity"). It's divided exactly in
  `ReceiptAllocationEngine.divideUnits` and mirrored on the server; the parity fixture
  `units-and-quantities.json` runs in both.
- **Review.** A share counts as reviewed only while it equals what the person reviewed. The host
  can finalize past unreviewed shares only by acknowledging each person in `FinalReviewView`; they
  show as host-resolved.
- **Conflicts.** `RemoteRoomLedger` resends a change after `revision_conflict` only when the item
  and room it depended on didn't change (`meansTheSame`); otherwise it explains. Payment steps go
  through `sendRemote`.
- **Shared code.** `SquareUpShared/` (wire types, `JSONValue`, invitation links and codes) is
  compiled into the app and the App Clip, so both read links and codes the same way.

### App Clip (prepared, not activated)

`SquareUpClip` is a separate target with its own small model and views (`ClipModel`,
`ClipRoomClient`, `ClipViews`) on top of `SquareUpShared`. It doesn't use the full app's design
system, ledger, or account. It joins a temporary room as a guest, claims, reviews, reports a
payment, and handles the endings. It isn't embedded in the app and has no team, Associated Domains,
or App Clip experience; see `APP_CLIP_ACTIVATION.md` for the ten activation steps and the measured
size. `AppClipUITests` drives its DEBUG build against a local server.

## Phone sign-in (October 2026)

Welcome keeps its two choices. **Create account** and **Sign in** each open a phone screen first
(`PhoneEntryView`); "Use email instead" swaps in the existing email form on the same route, so Back
always returns to Welcome and what was typed survives the switch. Continue sends a six-digit code
(`PhoneCodeView`); a new number then chooses its name (`ProfileNameView`, with a note when it came
from Sign in), a known one opens the app. Profile → Account → Sign-in methods adds a number to the
signed-in account.

### One session for both kinds of account

- **Firebase SDK, narrowly.** `FirebasePhoneVerifier` is the only Firebase SDK user:
  `PhoneAuthProvider.verifyPhoneNumber` (silent APNs verification, or Firebase's reCAPTCHA sheet) and
  `Auth.signIn(with:)` with the phone credential. Its result (UID, ID token, refresh token, number,
  new-or-not) is handed to `AccountSession.adopt`, and the SDK's own session is signed out at once.
- **`AccountSession` stays the session.** The refresh token goes in the same Keychain record as an
  email account's, ID tokens refresh through Secure Token REST, and `Authorization: Bearer` works the
  same, so sign-out, refresh, the per-UID local space (`AppSession`), account switching, and relaunch
  need no phone-specific code. The record gains the verified number (E.164); `isAnonymous` and
  `isRecoverable` count it.
- **Linking.** `AccountSession.linkPhone` sends the SDK-issued verification ID and the code to
  `accounts:signInWithPhoneNumber` with the user's ID token and `operation: LINK` — the request the
  SDK's own `link(with:)` makes — so the number joins the same Firebase user. A number that belongs
  to another account comes back as a temporary proof instead of tokens: refused as `phoneInUse`,
  never merged. A response for any other UID is refused too.
- **Configured or off.** Firebase is configured from Info.plist values filled from
  `Remote.xcconfig` (committed client identifiers; `Remote.local.xcconfig` may override them; no
  `GoogleService-Info.plist` in the bundle), and only when the reCAPTCHA
  callback scheme (`app-<encoded app ID>`) is registered — Firebase calls `fatalError` without it.
  Otherwise `FirebasePhoneVerifier.shared` is `nil`, `LiveAuthBackend` has no verifier, and both
  Welcome choices open the email forms as before. Setup: `PHONE_AUTH_SETUP.md`.

### The phone flow (`PhoneSignIn`, owned by `AuthCoordinator`)

- Numbers: `PhoneNumbers` (PhoneNumberKit's libphonenumber metadata) — every country with a
  searchable picker, formatting as typed, a pasted `+44…` choosing its country, validation, and E.164
  as the only form sent or stored. Firebase has the final say on whether a number can get a code.
- Sending: one request at a time; Square Up's own throttle (`PhoneSendThrottle`: 60 s between codes,
  five an hour, times only — never numbers) on top of Firebase's; the cooldown is shown. Continuing
  again with the same number reuses the code already sent.
- Code: six boxes over one `.oneTimeCode` field (AutoFill from Messages, paste, focus that moves as
  digits arrive), verify on the sixth digit, Change number, wrong / expired / too-many / quota /
  offline / cancelled each in plain words that never say whether a number has an account.
- Resuming: the verification ID (never the code) is kept in the Keychain (`PendingPhoneVerification`),
  so returning from Messages — even after iOS closed the app — reopens code entry for 15 minutes.
- Privacy: phone screens cover themselves in the app switcher; logs carry only Firebase's error code;
  the screens say Firebase/Google processes the number to send codes and prevent abuse.
- `SquareUpAppDelegate` (via `UIApplicationDelegateAdaptor`) forwards the APNs token and Firebase's
  silent notification; swizzling is off. `SquareUpApp.open` hands only `app-…` redirects to Firebase,
  ahead of `squp://` and invitation links, which are untouched.

### The server

`identity.py`: a token whose `phone_number` (E.164) is also one of `firebase.identities.phone` is a
verified identity (`Principal.verified` = verified email **or** verified phone). Rooms with an account,
shared groups, and AI processing check `verified`; anonymous accounts stay refused; `/v1/account`
adds `phoneVerified` and `verified`. The server never sends or checks codes.

### Tests

`PhoneNumbersTests`, `PhoneSignInTests` and `PhoneThrottleTests` (flow, cooldown, reuse, resume,
sign-out, linking conflicts), `AccountSessionPhoneTests` and `PhoneSessionBridgeTests` (adoption,
REST linking, the URL hand-off), `PhoneAuthSnapshotTests`, `PhoneAuthUITests` (DEBUG stand-in with
fictional numbers and the fixed code 123456 — no SMS), and the server's `test_phone_identity.py`.

## Direct invitations ("Find a person", October 2026)

Anyone in a shared group can invite someone who already uses Square Up by an email address or phone
number they know. The invitation waits in that person's Groups tab ("Maya invited you to Dinner
Squad", Accept or Decline). The group doesn't change until they accept: they aren't listed, see no
ledger or balances, and don't count toward the 25. Link, QR, and code invitations are unchanged and
remain the owner's.

### The server

- **Directory.** `AccountService.sync_directory` keeps one `AccountDirectory` record per *verified*
  identifier (the email Firebase confirmed, the phone number it verified), keyed by
  `SHA-256("squareup.directory.v1|<kind>|<normalized value>")` and holding only the account's UID.
  It runs when the app reads `GET /v1/account` (every launch) and when the name is saved, and only
  for accounts with a profile. An identifier that stops being verified on an account, or is
  verified by another account, stops finding the first one. The raw address or number is never
  stored or logged; it reaches the server only in the ID token and in lookup bodies.
- **Records.** `GroupDirectInvitation`, keyed by `<groupId>:<recipient uid>` (at most one per person
  per group, which is what makes a duplicate impossible), indexed by group, recipient, and expiry.
  States: pending → accepted / declined / cancelled, or expired after 30 days. A pending one also
  ends when the group is archived, its sender is no longer in the group, or the person joins some
  other way (derived when read, so no command has to touch invitation records).
- **Rules.** Looking up and inviting need an active membership in a group that isn't archived.
  Inviting yourself, an existing member, someone already invited, or into a full group is refused.
  Only the recipient can answer (anyone else's ID looks like one that doesn't exist); only the
  sender or the owner can cancel. Accepting is exactly once and reuses the code invitation's join
  (`GroupService._new_member` / `_record_join`): a new seat, a `memberJoined` event, the membership.
- **Enumeration.** No account, an unverified one, and one without a name all answer the same
  `{"match": null}`. Lookups (and invitations, which look the person up again) are limited per
  account to 10 a minute and 60 a day. Identifiers travel in POST bodies, never URLs. A match shows
  only the chosen name and a masked identifier (`s•••@example.com`, `•••• 3434`).

### The app

- **Inviting.** People has **Find a person** for everyone (members get an "Invite someone" section;
  the owner gets it under Invite people). `GroupInviteSheet` gains a mode: the owner switches between
  "Link or QR code" (unchanged; still the default from Invite people) and "Find a person", and a
  link is made only when that mode is shown. `FindPersonView` / `FindPersonModel`:
  - Email or phone number. The input is checked before anything is sent (`PersonIdentifier`:
    lower-cased email; E.164 phone via PhoneNumberKit, in the iPhone's region unless it starts with
    `+` or `00`).
  - The result shows the name and masked identifier, with **Invite to group** only when the person
    can be invited. Otherwise it says why: already invited, already in the group, or that's you.
  - Below the search, the invitations you sent are listed (the owner sees everyone's), with Cancel
    while they wait.
- **Answering.** `SharedGroupsService.receivedInvitations` is read with the group list (on Groups,
  pull to refresh, and the 20-second sync while the app is active), so an invitation from another
  iPhone appears without a push.
  - `ReceivedInvitationsSection` shows the invitations at the top of Groups, and the Groups tab
    shows their count as a badge.
  - Accept joins through the server, registers the snapshot like a code invitation, and opens the
    group. Decline asks first.
  - Everything is cleared when the account closes. A server without these routes leaves the list
    empty.
- **Development accounts.** `Dev <id>` is findable as `<id>@squareup.test`. `-SUDevUserID <id>`
  (DEBUG) fixes the app's development identity, so UI tests know its address.

### Tests

- Server: `test_direct_invitations.py` covers the directory, neutral answers, malformed input,
  authorization, rate limits, duplicates, accept/decline/cancel/expiry, the sender leaving,
  archiving, a full group, cleanup, log privacy, and two accounts in sync. It runs in memory and
  against the Datastore emulator.
- App unit tests: `DirectInvitationTests` (input checks, wire, service, the model) and
  `DirectInvitationSnapshotTests`.
- Live and UI tests (opt-in, real development server):
  - `LiveSharedGroupTests.directInvitations` drives two (three) Swift clients.
  - `DirectInvitationUITests` runs on one simulator, with the other people over HTTP.
  - `DirectInvitationTwoDeviceUITests` puts two simulators on two accounts: find, invite, accept in
    Groups, and see a shared expense on both.

## Dusk design system (October 2026)

The interface was rebuilt around one backdrop — light burgundy falling through navy into black
("dusk", `SUDusk` tokens in `SUColor.swift`, drawn by `SUDuskBackdrop`) — with Liquid Glass for
controls. Functional behaviour, identifiers, and the server are unchanged.

### Decisions

- **Dark only.** `squareUpTheme()` fixes the colour scheme to dark; the dusk gradient has no light
  reading. Light variants stay in the asset catalog (and `ContrastTests` still checks them) but
  never render. Every screen gets the backdrop through `suScreenBackground()` (the calm style:
  glow behind the navigation bar, near-black where content sits). Welcome, launch, and setup
  complete use the hero style with the animated signal field radiating from the logo.
- **Tokens.** The accent is a light blush (`#D9AEB3`) for tint, toggles, and selection; primary
  actions are bright white glass, not the accent. Surfaces are navy-tinted; `SUColor.glassFill`,
  `glassFillElevated`, `glassFillPressed`, and `hairline` are the translucent layers that sit on
  the backdrop. Money direction keeps its semantic colours, always with a sign and words.
- **Layers.** `SUCard` and `suSurface(_:)` are translucent with a hairline edge (never Liquid
  Glass — figures never sit on a moving blur). `suListRows()` gives `List`/`Form` sections the
  same surface. Glass is reserved for controls: `.suPrimary` (white) and `.suSecondary` (clear)
  capsules, `SUSegmentPicker` (a glass track whose selected segment morphs between positions),
  quick-action tiles, filter chips, and the floating action bars.
- **Material.** `SUBevel` is the panel material everywhere (cards, panels, tiles, list surfaces):
  a faint top-lit fill and a hairline that is brighter along the top edge; Increased Contrast
  swaps it for a solid edge. Large panels use `SURadius.panel` (36); inner layers are concentric.
- **Wallet (`SUWallet.swift`).** Home's hero: the groups that need you are lacquered cards
  (`SUWalletCard`: three dusk finishes, brushing, sheen, lit rim; neighbours never share a finish)
  peeking out of a stitched, grained pocket whose content is `SUBalanceCard` (the statement
  pill, the figure, the group count). Cards rise out of the pocket on first appearance and slip
  back as the hero scrolls away; under Reduce Motion they simply sit in place. The visible strip
  of each card is a link to its group (zoom transition source). An empty wallet shows a dashed
  slot. `SUOpenBalancesCard` sits below with `SUSplitMeter` (lit bar = owed to you, ticks = you
  owe), always with both figures and a sentence.
- **Covers and tabs (`SUPanels.swift`, `SUTextures.swift`).** `SUCoverPanel` insets a cover —
  `SUCoverArt`: the aurora, a glow at the focus, `SUHalftone`, and the subject's symbol pressed
  into the corner — in a panel, with content centred beneath. Covers carry an `SUCoverChip`
  readout (an `SUDotMatrix` figure or a symbol, plus two lines), an `SUAvatarCluster`, and a glass
  `SUTagPill`. Used for group detail, expense detail, settle up, and Profile. `SUTabbedPanel`
  is a panel with a raised folder tab sized to its label: frosted when it rises out of a cover
  (Pay: "You pay Maya"; payment status: the state on the tab), bevelled on the backdrop (the
  recipient's "Waiting for you" confirmation).
- **Smaller pieces.** `SUHeadline` (two-tone title), `SUActionTile` (glass tile, symbol in a
  disc; Home's three actions), `SUPanelSection` (a section's header and rows in one panel),
  `SUStatsStrip` (a capsule of facts, with a compact one-line fallback), `.suPill` and
  `.suCircle` (small glass actions), and `SUTransferMark` (`SUBlobShape`: the payer in the head
  of a drop whose tail points at the recipient, with name pills when needed). Hero and large
  figures lead with their whole units; the currency symbol and minor units recede
  (`SUAmountView(emphasizesWholeUnits:)`), and the spoken amount is unchanged. Display-only
  values come from the models (`NetPosition.owedShare`, `GroupDetailSummary.totalSpent`).
- **Styles render in views.** A button style that forwards to another must not read
  `@Environment` in the forwarded style struct (it is never resolved there); `SUGlassButtonStyle`
  draws through a view so enabled state and Reduce Motion always apply.
- **Motion.** `suRise(_:)` lets a screen's sections rise in once, staggered by importance, and
  adds a light scroll transition; both collapse under Reduce Motion. Mesh heroes
  (`SUMeshBackground`) drift slowly and pause offscreen, in Low Power Mode, and when inactive.
- **Visual QA.** `-SUOpen squp://…` (DEBUG) opens any deep link once the app is ready, so a tab or
  group can be screenshotted without tapping through.
